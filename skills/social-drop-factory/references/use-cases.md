# Social Drop Factory — Use Cases

This reference helps Hermes decide whether the Social Drop Factory is the right delivery pattern and which campaign type to use.

## 1. Recurring brand content

**Problem:** A business posts disconnected graphics with no weekly narrative or measurable next step.

**Pattern:**

```text
Monday: belief / category / education
Wednesday: story / demonstration / human proof
Friday: action / offer / event / conversion
                ↓
        one interactive Drop
```

**Best CTA:** book, visit, join, buy, attend, inquire.

**Primary metric:** CTA conversion rate, qualified leads, bookings, sales, attendance.

---

## 2. Personalized sales

**Problem:** Cold outreach, Loom links, PDFs, and generic proposals fail to feel specific to one buyer.

**Pattern:**

```text
prospect name + specific hook
short personal video
3 observations
what should change
relevant proof
one offer
book / reply
```

**Automation opportunity:** Generate hundreds of prospect-specific manifests from structured research while keeping one shared renderer.

**Do not:** invent audit findings or fake personalization. Observations must be grounded in inspected evidence.

**Primary metric:** reply, booked meeting, qualified opportunity, closed revenue.

---

## 3. Recruiting

**Problem:** Job posts describe requirements but do not let candidates experience the work or culture.

**Pattern:**

```text
belief / mission
what the work looks like
employee/team story
role
why it matters
apply
```

**Useful media:** team footage, day-in-the-life clips, manager introduction, customer impact, workspace, project examples.

**Primary metric:** qualified applications and completed applications, not raw clicks.

---

## 4. Local-service lead generation

Examples: roofing, plumbing, HVAC, landscaping, remodeling, auto services, salons.

**Pattern:**

```text
problem education
before / diagnosis
job or customer story
proof
clear local offer
estimate / inspection / booking
```

**Strong Drop types:** compare, reveal, before/after, checklist, short calculator, project story.

**Primary metric:** calls, estimate requests, booked appointments, qualified leads.

---

## 5. Nonprofit / community action

**Pattern:**

```text
why this matters
human/program story
what the organization actually does
proof / transparency
action
```

**CTA:** donate, volunteer, attend, sponsor, share, register.

**Risk:** Do not fabricate beneficiary stories, impact numbers, partner claims, or event details.

**Primary metric:** donations, registrations, volunteers, recurring supporters.

---

## 6. Coaching / mentorship / sports programs

**Pattern:**

```text
principle
coach/mentor teaching moment
athlete/student story
exercise/program/event
apply / attend / book
```

**Primary metric:** applications, trial sessions, program enrollment, event attendance.

---

## 7. Product / ecommerce

**Pattern:**

```text
problem or insight
demo
use case
proof
buy
```

**Useful Drop primitives:** compare, reveal, video, choose, configurator-lite, FAQ, proof gallery.

**Primary metric:** add-to-cart, checkout, purchase, qualified product inquiry.

---

## 8. Motivational / creator media

**Pattern:**

```text
principle
personal or audience story
challenge / exercise
community invitation
subscribe / join / share
```

The goal is not generic quote-card volume. Each week should advance a recognizable worldview or body of work.

**Primary metric:** subscriber growth, community joins, replies, challenge completion, paid conversion.

---

## 9. Event campaign

**Pattern:**

```text
why attend
who/what will happen
behind-the-scenes or participant story
logistics
urgency
RSVP
```

Social cadence can lead into one canonical event Drop that stays updated while individual posts remain lightweight.

**Primary metric:** RSVPs and attendance.

---

## 10. Customer onboarding / interactive handoff

**Problem:** New customers receive a long PDF or scattered links.

**Pattern:**

```text
welcome / promise
what happens next
short walkthrough
resources
required action
support path
```

**Primary metric:** onboarding completion, activation, fewer support requests, time-to-value.

---

# Choosing the campaign type

Use this decision map:

```text
Need recurring public attention?      -> weekly-social
Need one buyer to respond?            -> personalized-sales
Need candidates to apply?             -> recruiting
Need local leads/bookings?            -> local-service-lead
Need supporters to act?               -> nonprofit-action
Need people at an event?              -> event
Need a buyer to purchase a product?   -> product-launch
Need a new client to activate?        -> client-onboarding
```

# Minimum intake

Before building, gather:

- client/brand name
- audience
- business outcome
- campaign type
- governing idea or problem
- source facts/proof
- available media
- CTA
- destination URL or lead action
- deadline / publishing cadence
- approval owner

If critical facts are missing, do not silently invent them. Build the structure with clearly marked placeholders or request the missing source.

# The offer

A strong commercial framing is:

> We run the brand's weekly content system: one coherent story, three dependable social moments, one interactive destination, and one measurable business action.

For personalized sales:

> We turn prospect research into buyer-specific interactive pitches without rebuilding a website for every prospect.

For recruiting:

> We turn the employer story into an interactive recruiting experience that shows the work before asking for the application.

# What this is not

- not a generic social scheduler
- not an AI caption spinner
- not a giant website for every campaign
- not a fragile Instagram puzzle-grid gimmick
- not Webflow lock-in
- not a substitute for real client facts, real proof, or art direction

The factory should reduce repetitive production work while preserving human-quality strategy, evidence, and visual judgment.
