# Creator Doctrine — One Person Business

Source basis: 25 newest long-form videos + all 10 Shorts available on `@One-Person-Business`, collected 2026-08-16. This is a benchmark, not a rulebook. `OBSERVED` means directly supported by the corpus; `INFERRED` means Hermes should test it before treating it as causal.

## 1. Niche selection

- **OBSERVED:** favor niches that support longer videos, western audiences, and older/high-spending viewers when monetization economics support it.
- **OBSERVED:** reject the assumption that low competition automatically means opportunity; low competition can mean weak demand.
- **OBSERVED:** use multiple channels and experiments rather than assuming one channel is permanent.
- **OBSERVED:** hunt recent outlier channels: relatively new channels with a small catalog but disproportionate views relative to subscribers.
- **INFERRED:** outlier discovery is used as a market signal before committing production resources.

## 2. Validation

The benchmark repeatedly argues for validation before scaling.

For faceless YouTube, test:

- are channels under roughly three months old getting strong views;
- do they publish consistently and receive repeat views across uploads;
- is the format not dominated by too many entrenched channels;
- are new channels still breaking out;
- is monetization plausible;
- can the niche support a meaningful revenue target.

Treat thresholds as hypotheses. Hermes should use current first-party and market evidence rather than hard-code the creator's numbers.

## 3. Idea and competitor research

- **OBSERVED:** study winning videos and outlier channels continuously.
- **OBSERVED:** extract reusable structures from successful hooks/titles rather than copying exact expression.
- **OBSERVED:** use existing winning packaging from other niches as inspiration and remix the structure for a new audience.
- **OBSERVED:** seek gaps in what successful channels have not yet covered.
- **OBSERVED:** maintain reusable hook/title framework libraries.

Hermes adaptation: model `structure + promise + psychological mechanism`, never copy scripts, thumbnails, voice, branding, or proprietary expression.

## 4. Novelty and research

- **OBSERVED:** add lesser-known facts, obscure details, surprising insights, and additional research so the script is not generic.
- **OBSERVED:** use deep-research tools and source material before script generation.
- **OBSERVED:** run a fact-check/rewrite pass after generation.

Hermes rule: novelty must remain source-backed. Do not invent obscure facts to satisfy a novelty quota.

## 5. Packaging

Recurring title families in the corpus include:

- tool + outcome/income + timeframe;
- numbered secrets/prompts/niches/opportunities;
- contrarian warnings;
- proof-led transformation;
- opportunity reveals;
- direct underperformance diagnosis.

Hermes should generate several packages before scripting and score them for:

- target viewer clarity;
- concrete promise;
- curiosity gap;
- specificity;
- discovery surface fit;
- title/thumbnail complementarity;
- monetization alignment.

## 6. Hooks and retention

Common devices:

- financial or growth proof near the opening;
- specific case study;
- counterintuitive claim;
- promise of a step-by-step framework;
- numbered progression;
- open loops to a later reveal/tool/process;
- demonstrations and screen recordings;
- repeated proof examples;
- explicit transitions.

Hermes should track which devices actually improve its own channel retention instead of assuming benchmark tactics transfer automatically.

## 7. Production philosophy

- **OBSERVED:** sustainable simplicity is preferred to brittle overproduction: "simple scales" is a recurring principle.
- **OBSERVED:** faceless production can use AI for scripting, voice, visuals, editing assistance, SEO, and workflow execution while the operator retains strategy and quality control.
- **OBSERVED:** screen-recording/tutorial formats are heavily used for tool/process education.

Hermes adaptation: automate repetitive production, but preserve independent QA and originality.

## 8. Sollo workflow

The corpus directly shows a Sollo content workflow:

1. enter the Content area;
2. open the YouTube Script Writer;
3. describe the planned video;
4. supply a working title;
5. add unique subject details/research;
6. add reference YouTube links for structural style modeling when useful;
7. select a monetization goal;
8. choose/increase target length;
9. generate a script;
10. continue into voiceover and related production tooling.

Hermes uses Sollo as an execution provider after evidence, package, and strategy are already decided.

## 9. Monetization doctrine

Observed monetization paths include:

- YouTube ad revenue;
- affiliate/referral deals;
- sponsorships;
- digital products;
- communities/courses;
- services/agencies;
- software/tools;
- newsletters/owned audiences.

A repeated benchmark idea is to care about revenue/value per view, not views alone. Hermes should therefore attach a monetization intent to every channel thesis and video experiment.

## 10. Distribution doctrine

- **OBSERVED:** distribution is treated as a core business advantage, not an afterthought.
- **OBSERVED:** consistent organic publishing compounds over long periods.
- **OBSERVED:** YouTube can feed products, communities, services, and owned audiences.

Hermes adaptation: each successful long-form asset should be evaluated for Shorts, social, newsletter, and next-video/session derivatives.

## 11. Business-building principles relevant to the channel OS

The benchmark repeatedly advocates:

- solve real problems;
- match opportunity to existing knowledge/obsession;
- niche down further than feels comfortable;
- validate before building;
- talk to real customers/audiences;
- sell/validate before automating a business offer;
- automate only after the workflow is understood;
- focus human effort on decisions while agents handle repeatable work.

These principles align with Hermes ICM and should be applied to channel creation: validate the niche and package before investing in a production factory.

## 13. What Hermes must NOT adopt blindly

Do not automatically adopt:

- deletion of underperforming videos after a fixed number of days;
- creator-provided income/RPM claims as verified facts;
- fixed demographic rules for every niche;
- fixed thresholds for outlier validation;
- aggressive title language when it weakens trust or violates the target brand;
- close competitor imitation.

Use those as hypotheses and test them against current first-party data.

## 14. Operational synthesis

The benchmark's reusable process is:

`find evidence of demand -> identify under-served angle -> model proven packaging -> add novel research -> package before script -> generate with AI -> fact-check -> produce simply -> publish consistently -> monetize intentionally -> measure -> repeat`

Hermes adds stronger governance:

`provenance + independent QA + owner approval gates + rollback + first-party analytics + learning graph`.
