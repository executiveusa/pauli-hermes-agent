# Cinematic Taste System

Taste here is a decision discipline, not a list of fashionable looks. Hermes should be able to explain why a shot, cut, sound, or movement exists.

## Priority Stack

Judge decisions in this order:

1. **Audience emotion / comprehension** — what should the viewer feel or understand now?
2. **Story / information** — what changes because this shot exists?
3. **Rhythm** — is the duration appropriate to the moment and format?
4. **Attention / eye path** — is the viewer looking where the cut expects?
5. **Screen continuity** — are direction, geography, scale and graphic relationships coherent?
6. **Physical continuity** — are people, objects and space believable across the cut?

Do not sacrifice the top of the stack to rescue the bottom.

## Shot Test

Before approving a shot, answer:

- What changed?
- What does the subject want or do physically?
- What blocks, pressures or complicates that action?
- Where is the viewer positioned relative to the subject?
- Where should the eye land?
- What sound, silence or visual motif anchors the moment?
- Why this duration?
- Why this camera movement, if any?
- How does the shot hand the viewer into the next shot?

If the answers are vague, the shot is under-directed.

## Physical Specificity

Prefer observable instructions:

- weight shifts, hand placement, gaze changes, breathing, posture
- distance between subjects
- foreground obstruction or environmental pressure
- practical light source and direction
- lens/framing and camera height
- motivated dolly/pan/tilt/handheld behavior
- wind, fabric, smoke, water, reflections and other environmental motion
- exact sound event or intentional absence of sound

Avoid using generic quality words as substitutes for direction.

## Motion Discipline

Movement is meaningful when it tracks a change in knowledge, power, proximity, danger, intimacy or attention. Decorative camera motion should be removed when a stable frame creates more tension, clarity or authority.

Use stillness deliberately. A locked shot can make micro-expression, sound, blocking and tension more legible than constant motion.

## Continuity Bible

For continuity-heavy work, freeze these before production:

- face/body identity
- hair, wardrobe and accessories
- recurring props
- location geometry and anchor views
- time-of-day and key light logic
- palette / texture rules
- screen direction and character geography
- voice identity and pronunciation
- title/caption language

Record shot-specific changes explicitly instead of rewriting the whole identity prompt every time.

## Edit Rhythm

Use two passes:

### Rough Cut

Optimize for causality, comprehension, emotional progression and unnecessary duration. Remove shots that duplicate information. Do not add polish to avoid structural changes.

### Fine Cut

After structure works, refine entrance/exit frames, reaction timing, audio overlaps, music dynamics, transition shape, text timing, color and micro-pacing.

For short-form content, speed is not the goal by itself; **density without confusion** is the goal.

## Audio Taste

Treat audio as separate narrative layers:

- dialogue / interview
- narration / voiceover
- ambience / room tone
- foley / physical interaction
- designed SFX
- music
- silence

Do not let music cover weak structure. Silence may be the correct score for tension, truth, grief, scale or intimacy.

## Generated-Video Taste

A generated shot must still obey real editorial constraints:

- continuity into and out of the shot
- believable physical action
- no unmotivated camera wandering
- enough stable visual information for the viewer to read the beat
- a clear edit point
- audio that belongs to the pictured event

A generation that is attractive but unusable in sequence is a failed production asset.

## Reference Use

References should answer a production question: composition, blocking, lens behavior, color relationship, cut rhythm, lighting logic, character silhouette, title treatment, or sound texture. Never copy a reference merely because it “looks cinematic.”

When reference retrieval is available, search for the problem the scene must solve rather than for a superficial aesthetic label.

## Taste Review Score

Score 0–10 on:

- story/emotional function
- shot intentionality
- performance/physical specificity
- composition/attention control
- continuity
- edit rhythm
- sound design
- visual coherence
- factual/brand integrity when applicable
- format/audience fit

Recommended release average: **8.5/10**, with no critical integrity or playback failure.
