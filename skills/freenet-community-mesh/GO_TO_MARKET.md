# Go-to-Market: Seattle Sovereign AI

## Commercial position

We do not sell Freenet, a local model, or a box of open-source tools.

We sell a verified ownership outcome:

> The organization can continue operating designated AI workflows while retaining control of its data, credentials, models, backups, and exit path.

The Community Mesh is a bounded differentiator inside the existing commercial path, not a separate general-purpose SaaS product.

## Ideal first customers

Prioritize Seattle, Everett, and Skagit County organizations with:

- 3–30 staff or active volunteers;
- sensitive operational knowledge;
- repeated document, referral, outreach, or reporting work;
- no internal AI or infrastructure team;
- a clear local network of partner organizations;
- willingness to complete a two-week pilot and provide measurable feedback.

Best starting segments:

1. Small nonprofits and mutual-aid organizations.
2. Bilingual community-service organizations.
3. Youth and workforce programs without highly sensitive pilot data.
4. Independent professional and creative studios.
5. Community business associations and referral networks.

Do not start with healthcare treatment records, immigration case files, legal privileged material, children's personal data, or financial account systems.

## Offer ladder

### 1. Sovereign AI Readiness Audit

**Purpose:** determine whether local AI is technically and commercially viable.

**Scope:**

- machine and storage audit;
- data ownership map;
- vendor-dependency map;
- workflow selection;
- privacy classification;
- local versus cloud model plan;
- implementation and operating-cost estimate;
- rollback and exit plan.

**Proof delivered:** dated audit report, machine profile, one selected workflow, and a go/no-go recommendation.

**Suggested founding price:** $750–$1,500.

### 2. Sovereign AI Rescue Sprint

**Purpose:** take one existing cloud-dependent or manual workflow and make it owner-controlled.

**Scope:**

- one private knowledge base;
- local or owner-controlled model routing;
- one agent workflow;
- access policy;
- encrypted backup;
- operator training;
- verification and rollback drill.

**Proof delivered:** one real workflow completed under owner control with a recorded recovery test.

**Suggested founding price:** $3,500–$7,500.

### 3. Sovereign Launch

**Purpose:** establish the organization's local AI operating layer.

**Scope:**

- local AI node;
- document and knowledge indexing;
- model routing by machine capability;
- approved browser and software control;
- role-based agent boundaries;
- logs, backups, monitoring, and handoff;
- owner-controlled domain, hosting, storage, and credentials.

**Proof delivered:** acceptance test matrix, ownership inventory, live workflow demonstrations, and rollback evidence.

**Suggested founding price:** $8,000–$20,000 depending on hardware and workflow count.

### 4. Community Mesh Pilot

**Purpose:** connect two to five sovereign AI nodes through a deliberately limited shared-resource layer.

**Scope:**

- public/shared data workshop;
- CommunityResource schema;
- two-node simulator proof;
- optional non-production Freenet trial;
- publication and revocation approvals;
- leakage and tamper tests;
- operator interface and pilot report.

**Proof delivered:** two independently controlled nodes exchange an approved public resource while private records remain isolated.

**Suggested founding price:** $12,000–$30,000 total pilot, funded by a lead organization, cooperative, sponsor, or grant.

### 5. MAXX Sovereign Operations

**Purpose:** maintain, monitor, benchmark, patch, back up, and improve the verified system.

**Scope:**

- monthly health review;
- model and storage optimization;
- backup verification;
- security and permission review;
- workflow performance reporting;
- incident support;
- quarterly exit and restore drill;
- bounded improvement proposals.

**Suggested founding price:** $750–$3,000/month per organization, based on node count and support scope.

## Sales message

### Primary question

If your AI provider disappeared tomorrow, would you still own your organization's knowledge, workflows, credentials, and operating memory?

### Problem statement

Most small organizations are adopting AI by uploading more information into systems they cannot inspect, move, or continue operating without. They gain convenience but accumulate dependency.

### Outcome statement

We install and verify an AI operating layer the organization controls. Private knowledge stays local or in owner-controlled infrastructure. Cloud models can remain optional workers. Backups, permissions, and exit paths are documented and tested.

### Differentiator

For qualified Seattle partners, independently owned AI nodes can share selected public community resources without combining private databases under one platform owner.

## 30-day selling plan

### Week 1 — evidence and list

- Run the simulator and capture proof.
- Produce a one-page architecture diagram and audit sample.
- Build a list of 30 Seattle/Everett organizations.
- Identify one repeated expensive workflow per organization from public evidence.
- Contact 10 organizations with an audit-specific message.

### Week 2 — discovery and paid audit

- Complete five discovery calls.
- Offer three paid founding audits.
- Do not offer a free custom build.
- Collect the first payment before machine-specific implementation.

### Week 3 — deliver first audit

- Run `local-machine-architect`.
- Map ownership and dependencies.
- Select one Rescue Sprint candidate.
- Present quantified time, risk, and operating-cost findings.

### Week 4 — close sprint and recruit pilot partner

- Close one Rescue Sprint.
- Ask the first customer to nominate one trusted partner organization.
- Offer the Community Mesh only after the private node workflow is proven.

## Outreach template logic

Each message should contain:

1. One observed workflow or dependency.
2. One risk framed without fear marketing.
3. One measurable audit outcome.
4. A fixed-price paid next step.
5. No claim that the mesh is production-ready.

Example:

> Your organization appears to coordinate services across several tools and partner groups. We help small Seattle organizations identify which AI workflows can run under their own control instead of permanently depending on a cloud vendor. The founding audit maps one workflow, checks the available machines and storage, documents who controls the data and credentials, and produces a fixed implementation plan. The audit is paid and bounded; no software is installed without approval.

## Qualification gates

Do not sell the Community Mesh when:

- the customer has no proven local AI workflow;
- the shared problem is vague;
- participants cannot agree what data is public;
- no owner is responsible for each node;
- the customer expects regulated or sensitive records on an alpha network;
- there is no budget for setup and operations;
- the project would create a fourth active workstream.

## Success metrics

Commercial:

- first paid audit within 30 days;
- audit-to-sprint conversion above 30%;
- setup gross margin above 50%;
- monthly operations attached to at least half of launches.

Technical:

- zero private-record leakage in tests;
- 100% revocation synchronization in the pilot test set;
- recovery drill completed;
- owner can export and stop the system;
- operator completes the core flow without developer intervention.

## Claims policy

Allowed after simulator proof:

- "We have a deterministic prototype that separates private node data from approved shared resource records."

Allowed only after live two-node proof:

- "Two independently controlled Freenet test nodes exchanged an approved synthetic public resource and passed the documented isolation tests."

Not allowed until production-grade evidence exists:

- "Unstoppable"
- "Censorship-proof"
- "Impossible to hack"
- "Fully private"
- "Production-ready"
- "No downtime"
