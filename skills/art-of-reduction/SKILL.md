# The Art of Reduction
Version 1.0.0

## Purpose
Make the human's task easier without deleting what makes the work true, useful, distinctive, or safe.

**Full understanding → essential meaning → natural expression → smallest useful presentation → verified result.**

This is an editing and design workflow, not a mandate to make everything short, monochrome, or empty. The minimum is the point at which another removal would damage the intended outcome.

## 1. Inspect before editing
Read the actual material, its surrounding context, and the owner's instructions. For an existing product, inspect the relevant source, rendered experience, assets, content, and available tests. Read HEART_AND-SOUL.md when present. Do not overwrite it or silently install another doctrine.

Record an audit before proposing changes:
- **Object:** what is being edited; source path/version and format.
- **Human:** audience, task, device, language, and situation.
- **Outcome:** what the person must understand, decide, or accomplish.
- **Protected:** approved words, brand, mechanics, functionality, facts, accessibility, legal requirements, and ownership.
- **Evidence:** source material, measurements, screenshots, author samples, and what remains unknown.

If the user asks for an audit or approval first, stop after presenting the audit and proposed changes. Do not alter the target.

### Provenance is not an AI detector
Show the original human-written material and the AI-written material separately **only when authorship is known from the source, revision history, or user**. Mark other material `origin unknown`. Language patterns can be flagged as AI-like; they cannot establish who wrote a passage. Never invent a provenance label, a probability of AI authorship, or a human quote.

## 2. Choose the right editor
Use only the relevant branch. Read the upstream skill if installed; the local reference in this package is a routing summary, not a substitute for its complete rules.

| Object | Primary method | What can be removed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Letter, report, website copy, email | Humanizer + Stop Slop | Staging, repetition, inflated claims, generic phrasing, unnecessary explanations |
| Conversation, plan, instructions | i-have-adhd | Hidden state, choices, tangents, vague next actions, unnecessary memory burden |
| Website or interface | Collins protocol + ADHD + visual references | Duplicate sections, competing actions, decorative components, redundant controls |
| Brand, palette, typography, imagery | Strategy before styling + art direction | Unsupported colors, redundant styles, stock visuals, inconsistent effects |
| Flipbook, slide, fixed page | Content before containers + page-fit audit | Secondary ideas, repeated explanations, excess furniture; move evidence to notes/appendix |
| Workflow or code | Ponytail | Unneeded work, duplicate code, speculative abstractions, unnecessary dependencies |

For a small, clear edit, use the existing design system directly. Use Refero or other visual research when a genuine design decision remains unresolved. Do not create three or five design directions when the owner has explicitly locked one. Do not turn a fixed-book editing task into a new reader implementation.

### Non-negotiable boundaries
Never remove required facts, meaningful uncertainty, citations, safety instructions, accessibility, error recovery, validation, consent, approval gates, or legal obligations merely to reduce length. Preserve explicit requirements. Simplify the presentation or move detail behind an accessible secondary layer instead.

## 3. Run the reduction scale
These are **editorial levels created for this skill**, not a clinical ADHD scale or a validated psychological score.

- **R0 — Source:** Complete original material, evidence, and constraints. Immutable reference.
- **R1 — Essential:** Remove duplication and unrelated detail; retain every necessary claim and decision.
- **R2 — Human:** Rewrite in the author's voice. Replace formulaic AI patterns with specific, natural language.
- **R3 — Composed:** One dominant idea per page or screen; a clear hierarchy and next action. Fit the actual medium.
- **R4 — Minimum useful:** Remove anything else that can go without harming meaning, task completion, identity, trust, or accessibility.

Move forward only when the previous level still preserves the contract. R4 is not always the desired output. A legal document, technical specification, or complex decision may need R1 or R2 with complete detail.

### Subtraction loop
For each element ask: Does it need to exist? Can it be removed, combined, reused, inferred, remembered, prepared, or disclosed only when needed? Does it add a fact, aid a decision, express real identity, or support an action? Stop when another subtraction would make the work worse.

For code, prefer the existing implementation, standard library, native platform, and installed dependencies before adding new code. Fix the root cause. Never code-golf away maintainability or checks.

## 4. Show the edit before applying it
The audit output has five parts, each bounded:
1. **What I found:** source and current behavior, with known/unknown provenance.
2. **What stays:** facts, voice, mechanics, assets, and protected requirements.
3. **What goes:** exact examples of unnecessary material and why.
4. **Proposed reduction:** original → edited, with meaningful changes visible.
5. **Next action:** one approval question or one reversible action.

Use `KEEP VERBATIM`, `KEEP AND REPOSITION`, `REWRITE WITH APPROVAL`, `REPLACE WITH EVIDENCE`, `REMOVE`, and `MISSING AND REQUIRED` for content inventory. Preserve a complete audit trail outside the reduced client presentation.

Do not claim that text is human-written merely because the rewrite sounds natural. Do not invent a quote, statistic, customer reaction, date, identity, or outcome. Keep the author's distinctive language, including intentional repetition, emotion, dialect, humor, and punctuation. Remove a pattern only when it harms this specific text.

## 5. Verify and stop
Review the original and proposed version side by side. Check factual preservation, voice, comprehension, accessibility, medium fit, and the primary action. A shorter draft is not automatically better.

### Art of Reduction review
Score five dimensions from 0–10:
- **Meaning:** necessary facts, nuance, and intent survive.
- **Clarity:** the intended reader understands the point.
- **Humanity:** natural voice; no unsupported authorship claims.
- **Economy:** unnecessary words, elements, and decisions are removed.
- **Fit:** works in the real medium and preserves its constraints.

This is an **internal editorial rubric**, not a clinical or externally validated scale. A score below 8 in any dimension calls for revision. A score of 9 is the target, not proof of success. Missing required facts, broken controls, inaccessible content, or violated protected constraints are automatic failures regardless of average.

For substantial visual changes, use the independent review and release requirements in the owner's Collins protocol. Render the relevant breakpoints and inspect overflow, readability, navigation, focus, contrast, and reduced motion. For code, run the smallest relevant regression checks. A successful build is not proof of a usable product.

### Final delivery
Give the clean result, the smallest useful change summary, and any unresolved decision. Keep evidence and detailed rationale available separately. Do not add a sales pitch, ceremonial conclusion, or another round of editing merely to make the output shorter.

**Stop when nothing else can be removed without making the human less successful.**
