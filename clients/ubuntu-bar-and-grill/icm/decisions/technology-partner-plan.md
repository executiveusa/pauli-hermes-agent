# Ubuntu Bar & Grill — Technology Partner Plan

Date: 2026-08-27
Decision status: proposed operating plan; execute one experiment at a time

## Objective

Become Ubuntu's embedded technology and growth partner by increasing profitable revenue, reducing operating friction, and giving ownership a clearer view of what is working. Technology is used only when it improves a business outcome.

## Operating principle

We do not sell Ubuntu more tools. We become the layer that makes its existing tools, data, offers, customer relationships, and operating decisions work together.

Hermes + Ubuntu ICM becomes the decision memory. Existing production systems remain systems of record until a replacement is economically justified.

## The partner model

### 1. Revenue Control Tower
Create one weekly operating view from first-party systems and approved exports.

Track:
- total sales by channel
- direct vs marketplace sales
- average order value
- repeat-order rate
- rewards members and active members
- app installs and app revenue
- marketing-attributed orders
- catering pipeline and revenue
- Google local discovery actions
- review velocity and rating
- top menu items and attach rates
- capacity/slow periods

Value to Ubuntu: management stops making marketing decisions from anecdotes.
Value to us: we become the measurement and decision layer, not a replaceable content vendor.

### 2. Direct Revenue Engine
Keep DoorDash/Uber Eats for discovery while improving Ubuntu-owned repeat revenue through:
- direct-order conversion
- app/rewards adoption
- automated win-back
- abandoned-cart recovery
- occasion-based campaigns
- first-to-second-order conversion
- high-value customer segmentation

Primary metric: profitable direct repeat revenue, not email sends or app downloads.

### 3. Catering Growth Desk
Turn catering from a page into a managed pipeline.

System:
Lead -> response -> qualification -> quote/package -> booked -> fulfilled -> review -> repeat/account follow-up

Initial target segments to test:
- local employers and office lunches
- medical/clinic teams
- construction/trade crews
- churches and community organizations
- schools/nonprofits
- birthdays/family celebrations
- sports/team events

Metrics:
- qualified leads
- response time
- quote-to-book rate
- catering AOV
- gross margin
- repeat accounts
- revenue by source

### 4. Local Discovery + Reputation System
Unify Ubuntu's web identity around the current Everett business and systematically improve local trust.

Work:
- canonical business name decision
- NAP/category/hours consistency
- Google Business Profile hygiene
- marketplace/location naming cleanup where controllable
- review request workflow
- review-response standards
- customer-language mining
- service/menu issue escalation into operations

Primary metrics: discovery actions, calls, direction requests, direct visits/orders, review velocity, recurring complaint rate.

### 5. Menu and Offer Engineering
Use order-level data rather than opinions.

Analyze:
- item revenue
- contribution margin
- attach rate
- bundles
- first-time customer favorites
- repeat-customer favorites
- high-friction/value complaints
- items that travel poorly

Test:
- discovery sampler for first-time diners
- family/shareable meal
- high-margin side/drink attachment
- date-night bundle
- game/event package
- catering packages by headcount

No broad discounting without margin math.

### 6. Capacity-Aware Demand Generation
Before sending more traffic, map:
- busy and slow dayparts
- staffing
- kitchen throughput
- prep constraints
- delivery bottlenecks
- service/review failure points

Then shift campaigns toward profitable underused capacity rather than creating overload.

### 7. Customer Insight Loop
Every month, Hermes records:
Observation -> evidence -> interpretation -> decision -> experiment -> result.

Inputs:
- reviews
- order history
- campaign performance
- catering conversations
- lost leads
- customer questions
- menu feedback
- staff observations

This is the compounding asset. The longer we work with Ubuntu, the better the operating memory becomes.

## First 30 days

### Week 1 — Baseline, no unnecessary changes
Obtain read-only or export access to the systems Ubuntu already uses:
- Owner dashboard
- POS/order reporting if separate
- DoorDash Merchant
- Uber Eats Manager
- Google Business Profile
- Google Search Console / Analytics if present
- social accounts
- catering inquiry history

Produce a 90-day baseline for channel revenue, repeat behavior, local discovery, marketing attribution, catering, and capacity.

### Week 2 — Fix obvious leakage
Only after baseline:
- resolve business-name/location inconsistencies where controllable
- verify direct-order/rewards/app tracking
- confirm Owner automated campaigns are active and correctly configured
- create catering lead tracker and response SLA
- fix any broken/weak conversion paths found in the data

### Week 3 — Run one revenue experiment
Select exactly one based on baseline data.

Preferred candidates:
A. first-to-second direct order conversion
B. dormant-customer win-back
C. catering outbound + follow-up sprint
D. slow-daypart offer/bundle

One experiment only. Define baseline, target, audience, economics, stop condition, and result.

### Week 4 — Prove, document, decide
Report:
- incremental revenue
- incremental gross profit where data allows
- conversion movement
- operational impact
- customer response
- what we learned

Then choose the next experiment.

## Sticky without lock-in

We should be sticky because we create value, not because we hold systems hostage.

Ubuntu retains ownership of:
- domains
- accounts
- customer data
- ad accounts
- creative assets
- source code for custom work

We become difficult to replace because we maintain:
- clean operating data
- cross-system measurement
- experiment history
- customer/market intelligence
- revenue playbooks
- technical integrations
- decision continuity

That is legitimate embedded partnership.

## Commercial path

### Phase A — Revenue Baseline / Vibe Audit
Fixed-scope paid diagnostic.
Deliverable: revenue map, leak map, system map, three opportunities, one recommended experiment.

### Phase B — Revenue Rescue Sprint
Implement and measure the first high-confidence opportunity.

### Phase C — Embedded Technology + Growth Partner
Monthly operating cadence:
- weekly revenue pulse
- monthly executive review
- one controlled growth experiment at a time
- local/search/reputation stewardship
- marketing automation stewardship
- catering pipeline optimization
- data/tech integration maintenance
- quarterly technology roadmap

## Success test

We have earned partner status only if Ubuntu can point to business outcomes such as:
- more profitable direct orders
- more repeat customers
- more catering revenue
- higher average check or contribution margin
- fewer revenue leaks
- faster response/follow-up
- better local discovery
- better customer experience
- less owner time spent chasing disconnected tools

If we cannot measure at least one of those, the engagement is activity rather than partnership.
